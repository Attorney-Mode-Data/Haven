🖥️ Windows remote desktops get their modern graphics pipeline back. With H.264 on — the default since 22 July — Haven asked Windows for H.264 and said nothing else about how it wanted the screen sent, and Windows responded by hanging up on the modern graphics pipeline half a second into every session. The connection then ran on the old bitmap method: no H.264, no progressive refinement, no ClearCodec. Nothing announced it. The picture still arrived, just by the slowest route available, which read as "Windows is slower than other clients" rather than as a fault.

Found in two logs from the same phone and computer minutes apart — one at 33-51 fps, the other sending 24000 old-style bitmap updates instead. Reproduced against a Windows 11 machine here and narrowed to a single bit in one capability word: asking for H.264 on its own gets the channel closed, asking for it alongside any other option is accepted. Why Windows refuses the one and not the other is genuinely unexplained, so this ships as measured behaviour rather than as a theory. It may also be why one reporter saw a black screen with H.264 on in July. KDE and other Linux servers were never affected. (#477, #418, #425)

🔎 A remote desktop that discards half the picture now says so. A VirtualBox session threw away 53915 screen updates — 51% of everything the server sent — logging only "invalid declared destination" over and over, naming neither the region nor which of three rules rejected it. Now reports both, rate-limited instead of filling the log. No visible change; this is what makes the next report solvable. (#422)
